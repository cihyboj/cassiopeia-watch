Cassiopeia Watch privacy policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026. Applies to Cassiopeia Watch (package com.beamburst.casswatch), version 40.10 and later.

Cassiopeia Watch is an unofficial, open-source app for Casio Bluetooth watches, developed by volunteers. It is not affiliated with Casio. This policy describes what the app does with data, based on its source code.

Summary

The app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no server of its own. It does not collect, sell or share personal data. Its data stays on your phone, is sent to your watch over Bluetooth, or goes to an NTP time server if you turn that option on.

Bluetooth and your watch

The app scans for, pairs with and connects to your watch using Bluetooth Low Energy and the Android Companion Device Manager. It exchanges data with the watch to do what you ask: time and time zone, alarms and alarm names, hourly signal, watch settings, calendar reminders, timer values, button presses and battery level. On watches with a step counter (ABL-100WE) the app also reads the step count, the daily step totals and distances, and the step goal from the watch. Data sent to the watch goes only to your watch. The app stores the Bluetooth addresses and names of your paired watches on your phone.

Phone alarm backup

This option is off by default. If you turn it on and the app has no confirmation that the watch received an alarm change, it asks the phone's clock app to set an alarm shortly before the alarm time. The request contains the alarm's time and name. What happens to it afterwards is up to the clock app; this app cannot remove alarms it handed over.

Calendar

If you grant calendar permission (requested when you open the Events tab), the app reads, from calendars on your phone, the title, start and end time, all-day flag and recurrence rule of events from today up to two years ahead. On watches that support reminders, the app refreshes this list and writes it to the watch automatically: when the watch reconnects (automatic sync), when you press the watch's normal connection button, and when you send events from the Events tab. You do not have to pick events first.

The first five events in the list are enabled by default; you can change that in the Events tab. The watch has five reminder slots. Enabled events fill them first, and if fewer than five are enabled, the library used by the app fills the unused slots with data from disabled events. So events you have not switched on can still be written to the watch. What is written is the title, the date and time, and the recurrence. The app does not upload calendar data anywhere else.

Notifications

The app does not read your phone's notifications. It does not request notification access and does not forward notifications to the watch.

Location permission

On Android 11 and older, Android requires location permission before an app can receive Bluetooth scan results. The app requests it for that reason only. It does not read, collect or store your location. On Android 12 and newer the app uses the Bluetooth scan permission, declared as not used to derive location.

Network use

The app can query a time server using NTP (UDP port 123) to get a more accurate time to write to the watch. This is off by default and can be turned on in Settings. When on, the app contacts one server, pool.ntp.org by default, or a host name you enter. The server sees your phone's IP address, as with any NTP request, and the host name is resolved through your normal DNS resolver. The app does not send other data over the network.

Data stored on your phone

  • Settings, paired watch addresses and names, alarm drafts and cached watch settings, kept in the app's private storage (DataStore).
  • A sync history of recent operations for each watch (for example when time or alarms were sent, and whether it succeeded), kept in the same storage. You can clear it in the app.
  • Step history, if your watch has a step counter and "Step history" is on in Settings (it is on by default): daily step totals and distances, today's steps by hour and the step goal, kept for up to 400 days in a separate file in the app's private storage. It is not included in Android backup or in exported backup files. You can turn reading off in Settings; removing the watch from the app deletes its step history.
  • Crash logs. An uncaught exception can cause the app to write a log to its private cache directory. A log contains the app version, the phone manufacturer, model and Android version, the stack trace, and the stored watch addresses and names. Logs are not uploaded. On the next launch the app shows the latest log in a dialog only after a crash related to pairing; for other crashes the log stays in the cache directory and is not displayed. In both cases you decide whether to include any of it in a bug report.

Uninstalling the app removes this data.

Android backup

The app allows Android automatic backup. Per its backup rules, the DataStore files (settings, paired watch addresses and names, alarm drafts, sync history) are included in Android cloud backup and in device-to-device transfer. Step history and crash logs are excluded. Where the backup is stored, and who can read it, is determined by Android and your Google account or phone manufacturer settings. You can turn backup off in Android settings.

Exported backup files

In Settings you can export the app's settings to a JSON file in a location you choose, and import such a file again. The file contains the same stored settings, including the Bluetooth addresses and names of paired watches. The app does not send the file anywhere. Keep it private or remove those entries if you share it.

Diagnostics and sharing

The app never sends diagnostics or logs by itself. If you report a problem, you decide what to copy into a GitHub issue. Please remove Bluetooth addresses and anything personal first.

What leaves your phone

The developers receive nothing automatically: there is no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reporting service. No data is sold. Data can still leave the app along these paths, which you control or which Android controls:

  • Button actions. If you configure a watch button action, the app sends a broadcast intent to other apps on your phone (for example Tasker or MacroDroid) with the extras you configured. Any app on the phone that listens for that action can receive it.
  • Android backup. If automatic backup is enabled on your phone, the app's settings can be stored with your Google account (or your phone maker's backup service) and restored or transferred to a new device. See "Android backup" above.
  • Phone alarm backup. If you turn it on, alarm times and names can be handed to your phone's clock app (see "Phone alarm backup" above).
  • Exported backup files. A file you export is saved wherever the document provider you choose puts it, which may be cloud storage.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no personal data from anyone.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy are published in this file in the project repository, with the date above. For questions, open an issue at github.com/cihyboj/cassiopeia-watch/issues. Please do not post private data in public issues. To report a security problem, see SECURITY.md.
